# CTA Formulas Guide

## Purpose
This document defines practical CTA formulas for short-form ecommerce video.

In short-form commerce, a CTA is not just a closing sentence.
It is the moment where attention, belief, and desire must convert into an action.

This guide explains:
- what a CTA needs to accomplish
- the main CTA structures used in ecommerce video
- how to choose the right CTA formula
- how CTA strength should match persuasion level
- common CTA failure modes
- how to improve CTA performance in scripts, storyboards, prompts, and analysis

---

## 1. What a CTA Is

A CTA, or call to action, tells the viewer what to do next and why they should do it.

In short-form ecommerce video, this usually means guiding the viewer toward actions such as:
- tapping to view the product
- clicking into a shop page
- checking details
- buying now
- trying the product
- exploring options
- taking a closer look

A CTA should reduce friction at the exact moment the viewer is most ready to move.

---

## 2. Core Principle

**A strong CTA is not only an action instruction. It is an action instruction connected to motivation.**

That means a CTA usually needs to answer both:
1. What should I do?
2. Why should I do it?

Examples:
- “Tap to check it out.” → clear action, weak motivation unless context already did the work
- “Tap to check it out if you want that easy glow without the heavy feel.” → clear action + reason

The second is usually stronger because it links action to value.

---

## 3. CTA Formula Rule

A useful working formula is:

**Strong CTA = Clear Action + Reason to Act + Fit With the Proof and Message Before It**

If one part is missing:
- clear action but no reason → procedural, not persuasive
- reason but no action → suggestive, not actionable
- both present but disconnected from the video → weak fit
- strong wording but no proof before it → overreaching

The CTA is strongest when it feels like the natural conclusion of the video.

---

## 4. What a CTA Must Do

A strong CTA typically needs to achieve four things:

### 4.1 Clarity
The viewer should understand exactly what to do next.

### 4.2 Motivation
The viewer should feel there is a meaningful reason to act.

### 4.3 Friction Reduction
The CTA should make the next step feel easy and natural.

### 4.4 Context Fit
The CTA should match the trust, proof, and tone built earlier in the video.

---

## 5. Main CTA Formula Families

### 5.1 Action + Benefit
This is one of the most useful and versatile CTA formulas.

Structure:
**[Action] + [Main benefit]**

Examples:
- Tap to get that easy glow.
- Check it out if you want the faster version of this.
- Grab it if you want the lightweight option.

Best for:
- utility products
- beauty
- convenience-led offers
- broad direct-response content

Strength:
Simple, commercially direct, easy to understand

Risk:
Can feel generic if the benefit is too broad or weak

---

### 5.2 Action + Problem Relief
This formula ties action to the pain point the viewer wants to escape.

Structure:
**[Action] + [Problem relief]**

Examples:
- If you’re tired of dealing with dry-looking skin, tap to check it out.
- If you want to stop doing it the hard way, go take a look.
- Check this out if you’re over that heavy, sticky feel.

Best for:
- pain-first hooks
- problem-solution products
- categories driven by frustration or inconvenience

Strength:
Very strong relevance when the pain point is well chosen

Risk:
Weak if the problem was not clearly established earlier

---

### 5.3 Action + Proof Reminder
This CTA reminds the viewer of the proof they just saw.

Structure:
**[Action] + [Reference to proven result]**

Examples:
- If you want the one that actually gave me this finish, it’s right here.
- Tap to check out the one that made my skin look this fresh.
- If you want this result without the heavy feel, go look at this one.

Best for:
- testimonial content
- demo-led content
- categories where trust is built through visible outcome

Strength:
Makes the CTA feel earned because it connects directly to proof

Risk:
Weak if the proof itself was subtle or unclear

---

### 5.4 Action + Urgency
This CTA introduces timing pressure.

Structure:
**[Action] + [Reason now]**

Examples:
- Grab yours while the offer is still live.
- Check it out before the sale ends.
- Tap now if you want the deal while it’s still up.

Best for:
- discount windows
- promotions
- limited-time offers
- warmer audiences

Strength:
Can increase action if the urgency is real and already supported

Risk:
Feels forced if urgency is not justified in the video

---

### 5.5 Action + Identity Fit
This CTA frames the product as being for a specific kind of user.

Structure:
**[Action] + [Who this is for]**

Examples:
- If you’re the kind of person who wants glow without heavy skincare, tap here.
- If this fits your morning routine, go check it out.
- If you hate complicated skincare, this is worth a look.

Best for:
- niche audiences
- lifestyle categories
- products with strong audience segmentation

Strength:
Can feel highly relevant and personal

Risk:
Too narrow if the audience is broader than the identity framing

---

### 5.6 Action + Curiosity
This CTA keeps the ask lighter and more exploratory.

Structure:
**[Action] + [Open loop or curiosity reason]**

Examples:
- Tap to see the details.
- Go check it out if you want to see the full thing.
- Take a look if you want to see how it works.

Best for:
- colder traffic
- softer product education
- lighter-pressure creative
- top-of-funnel content

Strength:
Lower resistance, easy to accept

Risk:
May under-convert if the audience is already warm and ready for a stronger ask

---

### 5.7 Action + Trust Framing
This CTA uses the tone of recommendation rather than sales pressure.

Structure:
**[Action] + [Recommendation framing]**

Examples:
- If you want the one I’d actually recommend, it’s right here.
- If you want the version that’s been working for me, go check it out.
- This is the one I’d tell you to look at first.

Best for:
- UGC testimonial
- creator-led recommendation
- trust-sensitive categories

Strength:
Feels less aggressive and more human

Risk:
Weaker if the creator trust level is not established

---

## 6. CTA Selection Rules

Choose the CTA formula based on:
- audience warmth
- product type
- proof strength
- tone of the video
- business objective
- buying friction level

### Use Action + Benefit when:
- the main benefit is already clear
- the audience understands the product
- you want a clean conversion CTA

### Use Action + Problem Relief when:
- the hook led with pain
- the viewer strongly identifies with a problem
- the product solves an annoying friction point

### Use Action + Proof Reminder when:
- the proof is the strongest part of the video
- the result is visible and memorable
- trust still needs reinforcement at the end

### Use Action + Urgency when:
- there is a real offer or timing reason
- the viewer has enough trust to act now
- the business goal is immediate conversion

### Use Action + Curiosity when:
- the audience may still be too cold for a harder close
- the video is light, exploratory, or softer in tone
- the next step is discovery rather than immediate purchase

### Use Action + Trust Framing when:
- the creator voice is central
- the content relies on authenticity
- softer pressure will likely outperform hard selling

---

## 7. CTA Strength by Audience Temperature

### Cold Audience
Use:
- curiosity
- benefit-led
- pain-relief
- softer recommendation language

Avoid:
- overly aggressive “buy now” phrasing without enough trust

### Warm Audience
Use:
- benefit-led
- proof reminder
- practical direct action
- moderate urgency if justified

### Hot Audience
Use:
- direct action
- urgency
- offer-led CTA
- concise conversion-focused language

The CTA should match how ready the viewer is to act.

---

## 8. CTA Fit With Video Structure

A CTA should connect to what the video just established.

### If the video is pain-first:
Best CTA styles:
- action + problem relief
- action + benefit
- action + easier solution

### If the video is result-first:
Best CTA styles:
- action + proof reminder
- action + benefit
- action + curiosity to learn more

### If the video is comparison-led:
Best CTA styles:
- action + better alternative framing
- action + time-saving/value framing
- action + superiority reminder

### If the video is testimonial-led:
Best CTA styles:
- action + trust framing
- action + proof reminder
- action + benefit in creator voice

### If the video is offer-driven:
Best CTA styles:
- action + urgency
- action + value reminder
- action + limited-time reason

---

## 9. CTA Tone Rules

The CTA should match the style of the content.

### For raw UGC
Use:
- natural language
- recommendation tone
- easy phrasing
- conversational action prompts

Example:
- If you want that same finish, tap and check it out.

### For polished product showcase
Use:
- cleaner language
- more controlled benefit phrasing
- visually matched minimal CTA

Example:
- Discover the product here.

### For aggressive performance creative
Use:
- direct action
- stronger urgency if justified
- more compressed wording

Example:
- Grab yours now while the deal is still live.

### For creator testimony
Use:
- trust framing
- “this is the one I use” logic
- recommendation-led close

Example:
- If you want the one that’s been working for me, it’s right here.

---

## 10. CTA Positioning Rule

Most short-form ecommerce videos place the strongest CTA near the end.
But placement still matters.

### Good CTA placement:
- after the product is understood
- after enough proof exists
- after at least one meaningful benefit lands
- when the viewer has a reason to act

### Weak CTA placement:
- before the viewer understands the product
- before trust is built
- dropped in suddenly without connection
- repeated too early without increasing persuasion

A mid-video CTA can work in some formats, but only if it does not interrupt the core persuasion flow.

## 11. CTA Failure Modes

### 11.1 No Reason to Act
Example:
- “Tap here.”
- “Shop now.”

Problem:
The viewer knows what to do, but not why

---

### 11.2 Too Generic
Example:
- “You need this.”
- “Don’t miss out.”

Problem:
No product-specific motivation

---

### 11.3 Too Aggressive for the Trust Level
Example:
- “Buy now before it’s gone” after weak or no proof

Problem:
The CTA asks for more commitment than the video has earned

---

### 11.4 Detached From the Script
Example:
The video is about lightweight hydration, but the CTA suddenly pushes a discount without reconnecting to the core benefit

Problem:
The ending feels abrupt and less persuasive

---

### 11.5 Too Many Actions
Example:
- “Like, comment, save, share, and tap to shop.”

Problem:
Action overload reduces clarity and conversion focus

---

### 11.6 Fake Urgency
Example:
- “Act now” when no real timing reason exists

Problem:
Reduces trust

---

## 12. How to Improve a Weak CTA

### Improve clarity by:
- naming the action directly
- reducing extra wording
- removing multi-action confusion

### Improve motivation by:
- tying the CTA to the main benefit
- referencing the proof
- connecting to the original problem

### Improve fit by:
- matching the CTA tone to the video tone
- matching the CTA pressure to the audience warmth
- placing the CTA after the strongest proof moment

### Improve trust by:
- using creator-native language
- avoiding fake urgency
- sounding recommendation-led when appropriate

---

## 13. CTA by Workflow Stage

### In Content Analysis
Judge:
- whether the CTA exists clearly
- whether it is timed well
- whether it has a reason
- whether it matches the proof level
- whether it feels earned

### In Script Planning
Design:
- the action goal
- the reason to act
- the best formula for the audience and structure
- alternate variants if useful

### In Storyboard Planning
Support:
- a clear ending frame
- visual emphasis on the product or benefit
- readable overlay if needed
- a clean, actionable last scene

### In Prompt Conversion
Preserve:
- action clarity
- benefit or proof-linked ending
- scene-level support for conversion

### In Reference Adaptation
Check:
- what kind of CTA the reference used
- whether the same pressure level fits the new product
- whether the adaptation should keep the same CTA logic or change it

---

## 14. Practical CTA Checklist

Before approving a CTA, ask:

- Does it say what the viewer should do?
- Does it say why they should do it?
- Does it connect to the strongest benefit or proof?
- Does it match the tone of the video?
- Does it fit the viewer’s readiness level?
- Does it avoid unnecessary friction?
- Does it avoid being more aggressive than the content can support?
- Would it still make sense if read alone?
- Does it make the last moment of the video feel complete?

If several of these are weak, the CTA likely needs improvement.

---

## 15. Final Principle
A strong CTA is not just an ending.

It is the point where the video makes the transition from:
- “this is interesting”
to
- “I should do something about this now.”

The best CTA formulas work because they make that transition feel:
- clear
- justified
- low-friction
- natural
