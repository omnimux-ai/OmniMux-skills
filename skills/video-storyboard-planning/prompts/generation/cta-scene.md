# CTA Scene Generation Prompt

You are generating the **CTA / closing scene design layer** for a short-form ecommerce storyboard.

Your job is to design the closing section where the storyboard resolves into a purchase signal, action cue, or final persuasive landing moment.

This is not a full storyboard generator.
This prompt is specifically for the **CTA / ending section** of a storyboard workflow.

---

## 1. Objective

Generate a CTA section that does the following:

- lands the ad clearly
- converts attention into action intent
- keeps the product central
- feels like a natural payoff of the earlier beats
- remains commercially readable
- is easy to convert into storyboard shots

This section should answer the viewer’s silent question:

**“So what should I do now?”**

---

## 2. What a CTA Scene Must Do

A strong CTA scene should accomplish one or more of the following:

- make the purchase action clear
- reinforce the core benefit one last time
- leave a strong final product impression
- provide a clean closing visual
- create urgency or momentum when appropriate
- reduce hesitation before action
- align the product, result, and next step

A CTA scene is not just a text overlay.
Its role is to convert the accumulated persuasion into a final action-oriented beat.

---

## 3. Inputs You May Receive

You may be given:
- product name
- product category
- product description
- CTA wording
- ad goal
- target audience
- prior hook/demo/proof/lifestyle concepts
- product image interpretation
- video language
- style bible
- continuity anchors
- visual direction
- urgency or offer context
- duration target

Use what is available.
Do not require every upstream field if a responsible CTA scene can still be formed.

---

## 4. CTA Output Goal

You are producing a CTA design output that should help downstream systems create:

- 1–3 closing storyboard shots
- a clear action or purchase cue
- final packshot or final-use visual logic
- panel labels and beat roles for the ending section
- a closing sequence suitable for board rendering

Do not generate the full ad.

---

## 5. CTA Design Priorities

When designing the CTA section, prioritize in this order:

1. action clarity
2. product clarity
3. closing impact
4. continuity compatibility
5. visual cleanliness
6. urgency polish

Do not let decorative ending style weaken action understanding.

---

## 6. CTA Functions

A CTA scene may serve one or more of these functions:

- final product hero shot
- purchase invitation
- final benefit restatement
- urgency cue
- checkout cue
- packshot
- offer framing
- result-to-action bridge
- confidence close

Use only the functions the brief and runtime support.

---

## 7. CTA Families

Choose the closing family that best fits the product, sales goal, and sequence tone.

### 7.1 Product Hero CTA
Use when:
- the product itself should dominate the ending
- the product is visually recognizable and sale-ready
- a clean packshot style ending is appropriate

Pattern:
- centered product
- clean composition
- strong final benefit or CTA text

### 7.2 Result-to-Action CTA
Use when:
- the strongest close comes from the result the viewer wants
- showing the payoff and then action cue feels more natural than a hard packshot

Pattern:
- desirable result remains visible
- product is still present
- CTA lands as a natural conclusion

### 7.3 UGC Direct CTA
Use when:
- creator-native persuasion matters
- a person addressing the viewer feels more credible
- the ad tone is casual, social-native, or testimonial-like

Pattern:
- direct or semi-direct person-led cue
- product in hand or in frame
- short spoken or subtitle action line

### 7.4 Offer / Urgency CTA
Use when:
- the sales context includes urgency, offer framing, or time pressure
- the urgency is actually supported upstream

Pattern:
- clear action cue
- concise urgency support
- no clutter
- product still visible

### 7.5 Confidence Close CTA
Use when:
- hesitation reduction matters more than hype
- the product benefit needs one final practical reassurance

Pattern:
- clean final product visual
- subtle trust cue
- simple action instruction

---

## 8. CTA Selection Rules

Choose the CTA family that best fits:
- the product category
- the audience decision style
- the ad tone
- the likely purchase friction
- the amount of remaining time

Do not force urgency when none exists.
Do not force a direct creator CTA if the ad has been purely visual and product-led.

---

## 9. Product Fidelity Rules

The CTA section must preserve product truth.

Do not:
- beautify the product so much that it changes identity
- hide functional details the user has relied on earlier
- switch to a different packaging state without reason
- show unsupported accessories or bundled items as if included
- make the closing shot contradict earlier product appearance

The final impression should strengthen recognition, not reset it.

---

## 10. CTA Clarity Rules

A CTA is successful only if the viewer understands the intended next step.

The CTA should make clear, through image and/or text:
- what the product is
- why it matters
- what the viewer should do next

Depending on the ad, that action may be:
- buy now
- shop now
- tap to order
- learn more
- try it today
- add to cart

Do not bury the action inside visual clutter.

---

## 11. Visual Construction Rules

A strong CTA shot should usually have:
- clean composition
- strong product hierarchy
- limited distractions
- readable text placement
- a closing rhythm that feels intentional

Possible closing visual patterns:
- centered product hero shot
- product beside result
- product in hand with direct cue
- packshot with supporting props
- end card style product frame
- result payoff with subtle action text

Avoid:
- overbusy layouts
- too many props
- tiny unreadable CTA text
- excessive transition chaos
- abrupt tonal mismatch with prior shots

---

## 12. Audio Rules

Audio may support CTA with:
- short spoken instruction
- final subtitle cue
- music lift or resolve
- satisfying end SFX
- confidence-building closing line

Audio should help the landing feel complete, not rescue a weak ending.

All spoken and visible text must follow the video language.

---

## 13. CTA Timing Rules

The CTA section should feel like a proper landing, not an afterthought.

### Typical timing logic
- very short ad: 0.8–1.5s CTA beat
- standard 15s ad: 1.5–3.0s CTA section
- longer ad: 2–5s depending on structure

Do not end so abruptly that the action cue is lost.
Do not drag the CTA so long that momentum collapses.

---

## 14. CTA Guidance by Product Type

### 14.1 Appliance / Kitchen Product
Favor:
- product hero with result nearby
- practical desk or kitchen packshot
- simple buy-now close
- compact usability reminder

### 14.2 Beauty / Personal Care Product
Favor:
- clean product hero
- finish result + product pairing
- shelf or hand-held confidence close
- elegant but readable text

### 14.3 Home / Cleaning Product
Favor:
- clean-result payoff
- before/after resolution close
- product-forward practical ending
- simple reassurance + action

### 14.4 Fashion / Accessory Product
Favor:
- styled final look + product association
- wear-result close
- detail-led confidence close
- tasteful direct CTA

### 14.5 Gadget / Device Product
Favor:
- compact hero + utility context
- one final feature reminder
- portable setup close
- direct shop cue

### 14.6 Food / Beverage Product
Favor:
- appetizing final result + product
- craving close
- clean serve moment
- freshness / convenience final cue

- **Audio**: {audio}

- Do not fabricate a longer ending than the ad structure can support.

---

## 16. Beat Role Guidance

Useful beat-role values for CTA shots include:
- `CTA`
- `result`
- `proof`
- `detail`

Use the most precise role available.

In most cases, at least the final shot should be `CTA`.

---

## 17. Panel Label Rules

Panel labels must be:
- short
- clear
- action-oriented or structurally descriptive
- usable inside a storyboard board

Good examples:
- `立即下单`
- `产品定格`
- `结果收尾`
- `Shop now`
- `Final hero`

Bad examples:
- `This amazing product is waiting for you`
- `Do not miss this once in a lifetime offer`
- `The perfect ending to the best ad ever`

Keep labels compact and usable.

---

## 18. Continuity Rules

The CTA section must feel like a natural ending to the sequence.

That means:
- same product identity as earlier scenes
- same realism level
- same visual family
- no random environment reset unless intentionally designed
- no contradictory packaging, props, or result state

The CTA should resolve the ad, not visually restart it.

---

## 19. Negative Constraints

Do not:
- write the entire storyboard
- overload the CTA with too much copy
- invent pricing, discounts, or urgency not provided upstream
- turn the ending into a disconnected graphic slide unless appropriate
- hide the product behind decorative text
- introduce new product claims at the last second
- make the CTA so subtle that it no longer functions as a CTA

---

## 20. Quality Bar

A successful CTA output should feel:
- clear
- decisive
- product-forward
- easy to storyboard
- commercially useful
- consistent with the rest of the sequence

If the ending looks attractive but does not guide action, it fails.

---

## 21. Minimal Example

```md
## CTA Direction
Close with a clean product hero shot beside the finished smoothie so the benefit and purchase cue land in one readable final beat.

## Why This CTA Works
- It keeps the product central at the moment of action.
- It reinforces the result the viewer wants.
- It creates a clean and natural close for a short TikTok conversion ad.

## CTA Strategy
- **CTA Family**: Product Hero CTA
- **Primary Action Cue**: 立即下单
- **Primary Closing Benefit**: Fresh smoothie anytime with a compact portable blender
- **Suggested Duration Window**: 12.0–15.0s
- **Continuity Notes**: Keep the same blender body, fruit color palette, desk environment, and bright office-daylight logic established earlier.

## CTA Shot Candidate(s)
### Shot 1
- **Purpose**: Bridge result into action
- **Framing**: Medium close-up, front angle
- **Camera Move**: Slow push-in
- **On-Screen Content**: Finished smoothie glass sits beside the portable blender on a clean desk; both are centered clearly; the product is front-facing and easy to recognize
- **Audio**: 轻快音乐收束；字幕：“随时现打现喝”
- **Panel Label**: 结果收尾
- **Beat Role**: result

### Shot 2
- **Purpose**: Deliver final action cue
- **Framing**: Packshot, straight-on
- **Camera Move**: Static
- **On-Screen Content**: Blender hero shot on a clean background with minimal fruit props; product centered; bold readable CTA text: “立即下单”
- **Audio**: 音乐落点；字幕：“立即下单”
- **Panel Label**: 立即下单
- **Beat Role**: CTA
```
