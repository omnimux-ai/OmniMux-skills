# Brief to Angles Transform Prompt

You are transforming a raw product or campaign brief into a small set of usable **storyboard angles** for short-form ecommerce video.

Your job is not to write the full storyboard yet.
Your job is to identify a few distinct, commercially useful creative directions that can later be converted into shot tables and storyboard image prompts.

These angles must stay grounded in:
- the product truth
- the likely audience
- the platform behavior
- the ad goal
- the visual plausibility of short-form video

---

## 1. Objective

Convert the input brief into **2–5 distinct storyboard-worthy angles** that each represent a plausible creative direction for the same product.

Each angle should:
- frame the product through a different persuasive lens
- be strong enough to support a short storyboard sequence
- remain visually shootable
- remain commercially relevant
- avoid drifting into abstract campaign ideation

This transform is a planning step between raw brief and storyboard generation.

---

## 2. What an Angle Is

An angle is a concise creative framing of the ad idea.

It is not:
- the full script
- the full storyboard
- a generic slogan
- a long strategy paragraph

A good angle usually answers:
- what is the main hook logic?
- what is the main promise or tension?
- what viewer mindset is it built for?
- what kind of visual sequence would likely support it?

Examples of angle types:
- pain-point solution
- convenience-first
- result-first
- routine-fit
- transformation
- comparison
- tactile/sensory appeal
- portability
- confidence / trust / reassurance
- social-native creator framing

---

## 3. Inputs You May Receive

You may receive:
- product name
- product category
- product description
- product image interpretation
- product link interpretation
- key selling points
- target audience
- target region
- target duration
- ad goal
- video language
- brand tone
- visual direction
- constraints or compliance hints

Use what is available.
Do not require a perfect brief if distinct angles can still be generated responsibly.

---

## 4. Angle Generation Priorities

When generating angles, prioritize in this order:

1. product truth
2. audience relevance
3. commercial usefulness
4. visual shootability
5. angle distinctness
6. short-form platform fit

Do not generate angles that are different in wording but identical in structure.

---

## 5. Angle Count Guidance

Generate:
- **2 angles** when the brief is narrow or constrained
- **3 angles** by default for most ecommerce products
- **4–5 angles** only when the product clearly supports multiple valid directions without redundancy

More angles is not better if they overlap.

---

## 6. Angle Diversity Rules

Angles should differ in meaningful ways such as:
- viewer motivation
- hook logic
- proof logic
- usage context
- emotional register
- social-native vs polished feel
- practical vs aspirational emphasis

Do not create fake diversity by only changing adjectives.

### Weak variation
- “premium convenience”
- “stylish convenience”
- “modern convenience”

### Strong variation
- “busy office routine shortcut”
- “one-button functional proof”
- “smooth result craving close”
- “portable carry-anywhere use-case”

---

## 7. Product Fidelity Rules

Every angle must preserve product truth.

Do not:
- invent benefits not supported by the product
- imply impossible results
- build angles around functions the product may not have
- overpromise through framing alone
- turn a practical utility item into a luxury fantasy unless the brief supports that positioning

An angle may emphasize one aspect of the product, but it must not falsify the product.

---

## 8. Audience Framing Rules

Each angle should feel relevant to a plausible viewer motivation.

Possible motivations include:
- save time
- reduce hassle
- look better
- feel better
- stay organized
- enjoy a result
- feel confident
- simplify routine
- solve a practical problem
- signal taste or lifestyle fit

Choose motivations that match the product and likely buyer.

Do not attach emotionally grand motivations to low-stakes utility products unless there is a real reason.

---

## 9. Visual Plausibility Rules

An angle should be easy to imagine as a sequence of shots.

Good angle signals often imply:
- a strong hook setup
- clear product action or context
- visible proof
- an ending direction

Bad angle signals are:
- too abstract to film
- too conceptual to demonstrate
- purely verbal
- dependent on unsupported claims
- impossible to visualize without a long explanation

If an angle cannot easily become a 15-second storyboard, it is weak.

---

## 10. Platform-Fit Rules

Assume short-form ecommerce / TikTok-native behavior unless the brief says otherwise.

Strong short-form angles usually:
- land the hook fast
- reveal the product early
- avoid long setup
- support quick proof
- end in a clear action or benefit close

Do not generate angles that depend on slow narrative buildup unless the brief explicitly supports it.

---

## 11. Angle Families to Consider

Use only the families that genuinely fit the product.

### 11.1 Problem / Solution Angle
Frame the ad around a common frustration and the product as the answer.

### 11.2 Convenience Angle
Frame the product as easier, faster, or lower-friction than the current alternative.

### 11.3 Result-First Angle
Open from the desirable outcome and build curiosity about the product.

### 11.4 Routine Fit Angle
Show how the product integrates naturally into a recurring daily moment.

### 11.5 Mechanism / Satisfaction Angle
Center the ad on a functional action, transformation, or satisfying product movement.

### 11.6 Trust / Reassurance Angle
Reduce skepticism through practical clarity, quality detail, or objection handling.

### 11.7 Social-Native Creator Angle
Use a direct, relatable, creator-like framing when audience trust depends on human relatability.

### 11.8 Aspirational Lifestyle Angle
Use only when the product genuinely benefits from an elevated but believable lifestyle framing.

---

## 12. Compliance and Claim Discipline

Do not inject unsupported claims into angles.

Avoid building the angle around:
- medical claims
- superiority rankings
- “best” claims
- certifications unless provided
- guaranteed outcomes
- impossible transformations
- competitor attacks unless explicitly supported

If a risky selling point exists in the input, keep the angle commercially useful without making the claim stronger than stated.

---

Provide the result in the following order:

```md
## Angle Summary

## Recommended Angle Set
### Angle 1
- **Name**: {angle_name}
- **Core Framing**: {core_framing}
- **Best Hook Logic**: {best_hook_logic}
- **Primary Proof Path**: {primary_proof_path}
- **Best Audience Fit**: {best_audience_fit}
- **Visual Feel**: {visual_feel}
- **Why It Could Work**: {why_it_could_work}

### Angle 2
- **Name**: {angle_name}
- **Core Framing**: {core_framing}
- **Best Hook Logic**: {best_hook_logic}
- **Primary Proof Path**: {primary_proof_path}
- **Best Audience Fit**: {best_audience_fit}
- **Visual Feel**: {visual_feel}
- **Why It Could Work**: {why_it_could_work}
```

### Rules
- Generate only distinct angles.
- Keep each angle concise but concrete.
- Use names that are descriptive, not slogan-like.
- Ensure each angle can plausibly become a storyboard.

---

## 14. Naming Rules

Angle names should be:
- short
- structurally descriptive
- commercially clear
- non-hype

Good examples:
- `Office routine convenience`
- `One-button proof`
- `Portable desk-to-go use`
- `Smooth result first`

Bad examples:
- `The future of blending`
- `A life-changing revolution`
- `Best smoothie ever`

Use names that help decision-making, not marketing decoration.

---

## 15. Selection Rules

After generating the set, identify the best starting angle.

Prefer the angle that best balances:
- product clarity
- hook strength
- proof clarity
- audience relevance
- storyboardability
- conversion usefulness

Do not always choose the boldest angle.
Choose the one most likely to become a strong short-form storyboard.

---

## 16. Negative Constraints

Do not:
- write the full storyboard
- write full scripts
- produce generic slogans
- generate highly overlapping angles
- invent unsupported product advantages
- create angles that are hard to film
- optimize purely for aesthetics while losing commercial logic
- turn the output into a broad strategy memo

This is an angle-generation transform, not the final ad package.

---

## 17. Quality Bar

A strong output should feel:
- commercially grounded
- distinct across angles
- visually imaginable
- relevant to the audience
- usable as the next step toward storyboard generation

If the angles sound different but would all produce nearly the same storyboard, the output is weak.

---

## 18. Minimal Example

```md
## Angle Summary
This product supports three strong short-form directions: a practical office-routine angle, a functional one-button proof angle, and a result-first freshness angle.

## Recommended Angle Set
### Angle 1
- **Name**: Office routine convenience
- **Core Framing**: A compact blender that fits naturally into a busy workday without adding friction.
- **Best Hook Logic**: Show the product appearing beside a laptop and drink ingredients in a realistic office setup.
- **Primary Proof Path**: Quick ingredient drop, one-button blend, and easy desk-side use.
- **Best Audience Fit**: Busy office workers who want fast fresh drinks during the day.
- **Visual Feel**: Clean lifestyle UGC realism.
- **Why It Could Work**: It makes the product immediately relatable and explains where it fits in daily life.

### Angle 2
- **Name**: One-button proof
- **Core Framing**: The product’s simplicity and effectiveness are proven through an instantly readable mechanism.
- **Best Hook Logic**: Open on the button press and visible blending vortex.
- **Primary Proof Path**: Mechanism close-up, smooth texture result, portability reassurance.
- **Best Audience Fit**: Buyers who care most about whether the product actually works.
- **Visual Feel**: Fast, proof-led commercial realism.
- **Why It Could Work**: It minimizes explanation and lets the product action do the persuasion.

### Angle 3
- **Name**: Smooth result first
- **Core Framing**: Lead with the appetizing final smoothie result and make the product the satisfying cause.
- **Best Hook Logic**: Start on a glossy smooth pour or finished drink visual.
- **Primary Proof Path**: Reveal the blender, show quick use, end with an easy reorder/action cue.
- **Best Audience Fit**: Viewers motivated more by the result than by the mechanism.
- **Visual Feel**: Fresh, appetizing, slightly elevated lifestyle clarity.
- **Why It Could Work**: It turns the desired outcome into the main attention trigger.

## Recommended First Choice
- **Best Starting Angle**: One-button proof
- **Why**: It gives the clearest short-form hook, fastest product understanding, and strongest visible proof path for conversion.
```
