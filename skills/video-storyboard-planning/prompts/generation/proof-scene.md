# Proof Scene Generation Prompt

You are generating the **proof scene design layer** for a short-form ecommerce storyboard.

Your job is to design the section where the ad earns trust by making the product benefit feel believable, visible, and commercially persuasive.

This is not a full storyboard generator.
This prompt is specifically for the **proof / trust-building section** of a storyboard workflow.

---

## 1. Objective

Generate a proof section that does the following:

- strengthens belief in the product
- makes the claimed benefit feel concrete
- reduces skepticism
- supports conversion without over-explaining
- fits naturally after the hook or demo
- remains easy to convert into storyboard shots

This section should answer the viewer’s silent question:

**“Okay — but can I actually trust this?”**

---

## 2. What a Proof Scene Must Do

A strong proof scene should accomplish one or more of the following:

- verify a product benefit visually
- provide a reassuring detail
- answer a likely objection
- show a stable result
- show real-world usability
- increase trust through specificity
- reinforce that the product is not just visually appealing, but useful

A proof scene is not just another demo beat.
Its main function is **credibility reinforcement**.

---

## 3. Inputs You May Receive

You may be given:
- product name
- product category
- product description
- selling points
- product image interpretation
- resolved product data
- user script
- audience concerns
- hook concept
- demo concept
- target duration
- target viewer
- video language
- style bible
- continuity anchors
- CTA direction

Use what is available responsibly.
Do not require full upstream completeness if a sensible proof concept can be generated safely.

---

## 4. Proof Output Goal

You are producing a proof design output that should help downstream systems create:

- 1–4 trust-building storyboard shots
- visible reassurance after the hook/demo
- a stronger bridge toward CTA
- beat-role and panel-label candidates
- visual proof suitable for storyboard-image rendering

Do not generate the full ad structure end to end.

---

## 5. Proof Design Priorities

When designing the proof section, prioritize in this order:

1. credibility
2. visual evidence
3. product clarity
4. objection handling relevance
5. continuity stability
6. pacing efficiency

Do not confuse proof with spectacle.

---

## 6. Proof Functions

A proof scene may serve one or more of these functions:

- result verification
- durability or quality reassurance
- ease-of-use confirmation
- size / scale clarification
- material truth confirmation
- packaging / component clarity
- trust through repeatability
- objection handling
- practical realism
- “this seems usable in my life” reassurance

Use only the functions that the product and runtime can support.

---

## 7. Proof Families

Choose the most suitable proof family based on the product and the likely viewer skepticism.

### 7.1 Result Confirmation Proof
Use when:
- the result is visible
- the viewer needs reassurance that the outcome is real
- a stable finished state matters

Examples:
- smooth drink texture after blending
- clean surface after spray use
- neat organization after setup
- finished style after try-on or wear

### 7.2 Objection-Handling Proof
Use when:
- a likely practical concern blocks conversion
- trust depends on answering a specific doubt

Examples:
- leak resistance
- size portability
- easy cleanup
- ease of storage
- comfort
- durability gesture

### 7.3 Detail / Quality Proof
Use when:
- the product’s build, texture, finish, or components matter
- the audience wants reassurance about quality or authenticity

Examples:
- zipper detail
- material close-up
- packaging alignment
- button feel
- texture spread
- stitch or seam quality

### 7.4 In-Context Realism Proof
Use when:
- the viewer needs to see the product behave naturally in a real setting
- practical life fit is more persuasive than dramatic mechanism

Examples:
- used at a work desk
- tossed into a bag
- used in a bathroom routine
- placed in a car cup holder
- handled with one hand while multitasking

### 7.5 Comparison / Contrast Proof
Use when:
- a contrast helps trust
- a difference can be shown clearly and fairly

This must remain honest and visually grounded.
Do not invent unsupported superiority claims.

### 7.6 Repeatability Proof
Use when:
- trust increases when the action or result appears consistent, not lucky
- the product benefit should look dependable

Examples:
- second use looks just as smooth
- another angle confirms the same finished result
- repeated quick-use action remains easy

---

## 8. Proof Selection Rules

Choose the proof family that best answers the most important viewer doubt.

Ask:
- What is the viewer least likely to trust immediately?
- What would make the product feel more real or safer to purchase?
- What can be shown honestly in a short visual beat?

Do not use proof for claims that cannot be visually supported.

---

## 9. Product Fidelity Rules

The proof section must preserve product truth.

Do not:
- fake durability beyond what is implied
- show impossible results
- exaggerate performance visually
- add unsupported quality cues
- imply certifications, rankings, or claims not provided
- swap materials or construction details across shots

If the proof depends on product orientation or a visible component, preserve it carefully.

---

## 10. Credibility Rules

Proof works only when it feels believable.

Strong proof tends to be:
- specific
- visible
- grounded
- easy to interpret
- appropriately scaled
- connected to a real concern

Weak proof tends to be:
- too abstract
- too glamorous
- unsupported by action
- overexplained
- impossible to verify visually
- detached from the buyer’s doubt

When in doubt, prefer practical realism over theatrical persuasion.

---

## 11. Timing Rules

The proof section should be compact but meaningful.

### Typical timing logic
- very short ad: 1 compact proof beat
- standard 15s ad: 1–3 proof shots
- longer ad: 2–4 proof beats depending on complexity

Do not let proof dominate the whole ad unless the product requires heavy reassurance.

Do not rush proof so hard that nothing is actually believable.

---

## 12. Proof Shot Design Rules

Each proof shot should have one clear trust-building job.

Possible shot purposes:
- verify result texture
- clarify true size
- show easy cleanup
- show portability
- show quality detail
- show repeated successful use
- answer a practical objection

A good proof shot should:
- keep the product readable
- keep the proof target obvious
- avoid unnecessary motion clutter
- make the trust cue easy to understand

Do not stack too many trust functions into one unclear shot.

---

## 13. Audio Rules

Audio may support proof with:
- concise reassurance line
- satisfying material SFX
- simple subtitle reinforcement
- natural reaction
- soft confidence-building music pacing

Audio must not be the only place where the proof exists.

All spoken and visible text must follow the video language.

## 16. Beat Role Guidance

Useful beat-role values for proof shots include:
- `proof`
- `detail`
- `contrast`
- `result`
- `reveal`

Use the most precise role available.

Do not label every shot `proof` if another role is more accurate.

---

## 17. Panel Label Rules

Panel labels must be:
- short
- readable
- production-usable
- descriptive of function, not ad hype

Good examples:
- `细节近景`
- `顺滑成果`
- `随手携带`
- `Leak check`
- `Texture close-up`

Bad examples:
- `This proves it is the best`
- `Unbelievable quality reveal`
- `You need this right now`

Keep labels structural and compact.

---

## 18. Continuity Rules

The proof section must remain compatible with everything around it.

That means:
- same product identity as hook and demo
- same person identity where relevant
- same scene family or a believable connected environment
- stable lighting logic
- stable realism level
- no contradictory packaging or result appearance

Proof should reinforce the ad, not create new uncertainty.

---

## 19. Negative Constraints

Do not:
- write the CTA section
- rewrite the hook
- turn proof into generic beauty footage
- rely on unsupported claims
- create fake social proof
- overdramatize practical details
- insert competitor comparisons without support
- make the proof so subtle that it stops being proof

---

## 20. Quality Bar

A successful proof output should feel:
- believable
- specific
- visually grounded
- useful for conversion
- easy to map into storyboard rows
- strong enough to reduce buyer doubt

If the proof looks polished but does not increase trust, it fails.

---

## 21. Minimal Example

```md
## Proof Direction
Use a smooth-result close-up and an easy-carry reassurance shot to show that the blender is both effective and practical for daily office use.

## Why This Proof Works
- It confirms the blending result visually rather than just claiming it.
- It addresses portability, which is a likely purchase concern.
- It bridges naturally from mechanism demo into CTA.

## Proof Strategy
- **Proof Family**: Result Confirmation Proof
- **Primary Doubt Addressed**: Will the result actually be smooth and convenient enough for daily use?
- **Primary Evidence Type**: Visible result texture plus in-context portability cue
- **Suggested Duration Window**: 7.0–11.5s
- **Continuity Notes**: Keep the same product body, transparent cup, desk environment, and office-casual lead from earlier shots.

## Proof Shot Candidate(s)
### Shot 1
- **Purpose**: Confirm result quality
- **Framing**: Close-up, front angle
- **Camera Move**: Static
- **On-Screen Content**: Smoothie pours into a glass with even, glossy texture; no chunks visible; the blender remains beside the glass for immediate product association
- **Audio**: 字幕：“细腻不结块”；顺滑倒出的液体声
- **Panel Label**: 顺滑成果
- **Beat Role**: result

### Shot 2
- **Purpose**: Reassure portability and practical use
- **Framing**: Medium close-up, eye level
- **Camera Move**: Handheld follow
- **On-Screen Content**: User picks up the compact blender easily with one hand and places it beside a laptop and notebook, showing office-friendly scale and convenience
- **Audio**: 轻快音乐延续；字幕：“随时带走”
- **Panel Label**: 随手携带
- **Beat Role**: proof
```
