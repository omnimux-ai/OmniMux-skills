# Quality Checklist

## Purpose
This document defines the quality standards for outputs produced by the public UGC Commerce Video Skill package.

The goal of this checklist is to help evaluators, builders, strategists, and operators verify that outputs are not only complete, but commercially useful.

A “good” output in this system is not just well-written.
It should also be:
- strategically sound
- platform-appropriate
- visually or operationally executable
- product-clear
- persuasion-aware
- ready for the next workflow step

This checklist can be used for:
- prompt QA
- schema output review
- template validation
- creative review
- workflow testing
- agent evaluation

---

## 1. Universal Quality Standard

Every output should help the user move toward one or more of these outcomes:

1. stop the scroll
2. improve product understanding
3. increase trust or belief
4. reduce hesitation
5. improve action likelihood
6. support clean production or generation

If an output does not improve at least one of these meaningfully, it may be structurally correct but commercially weak.

---

## 2. Core Evaluation Dimensions

Use these dimensions for evaluating almost any workflow stage:

### 2.1 Commercial Clarity
- Is the output clearly tied to a commercial objective?
- Is it obvious what the content is trying to achieve?
- Is the product’s role in the content understandable?
- Is the message useful for conversion, not just creativity?

### 2.2 Audience Relevance
- Is the output shaped for a specific viewer or user type?
- Does it connect to audience pain, desire, or routine?
- Does it reflect the likely belief gap of the intended audience?

### 2.3 Product Understanding
- Is the product easy to understand?
- Is its function, result, or use case clear?
- Is product relevance established early enough?
- Is the product treated as the hero where appropriate?

### 2.4 Proof Strength
- Are important claims supported?
- Is visible or credible proof present where needed?
- Does the output avoid making claims that cannot be shown or supported?
- Does proof arrive early enough to matter?

### 2.5 Objection Handling
- Does the output address the most important likely hesitation?
- Is reassurance specific rather than generic?
- Is objection handling integrated naturally into the structure?
- Is the right objection being addressed?

### 2.6 CTA Effectiveness
- Does the output make the next action clear?
- Is the CTA connected to the strongest benefit or proof?
- Does the CTA fit the trust level built before it?
- Does the CTA avoid unnecessary friction?

### 2.7 Platform-Native Fit
- Does the output feel suitable for short-form, mobile-first consumption?
- Is the pacing aligned with feed behavior?
- Does the tone feel native rather than imported from traditional ad copy?
- If UGC-style, does it feel human and believable?

### 2.8 Execution Readiness
- Can the output actually be filmed, edited, briefed, or generated?
- Is it too abstract to execute?
- Does it contain the level of detail needed by the next operator or system?
- Is it practical, not just theoretically strong?

## 3. Workflow-Specific Checklists

---

## 3.1 Content Analysis Checklist

A strong analysis should not only describe the video.
It should explain how the video is trying to sell.

### Hook Review
- Does the analysis identify the hook type?
- Does it evaluate the first 1–3 seconds seriously?
- Does it explain clarity, relevance, and tension?
- Does it assess mute-viewing strength?

### Structural Review
- Does it break the video into commercial sections?
- Are sections labeled by persuasive function, not only by scene?
- Is the sequence of hook → reveal → proof → CTA made visible?

### Selling Logic Review
- Does it identify the main selling point?
- Does it separate features from benefits?
- Does it explain whether the message hierarchy is clear?

### Proof Review
- Does it identify what proof exists?
- Does it judge whether the proof is believable and relevant?
- Does it identify proof gaps?

### Objection Review
- Does it infer likely objections correctly?
- Does it explain which ones are handled and which are not?

### CTA Review
- Does it identify what action is being requested?
- Does it evaluate the CTA in context?

### Recommendation Review
- Are the recommendations specific?
- Are they prioritized by likely impact?
- Do they explain what should change and why?

#### Red Flags
- only describes scenes
- gives generic feedback like “make it more engaging”
- ignores proof or objections
- treats CTA as isolated
- skips hook analysis depth

---

## 3.2 Script Planning Checklist

A strong script output should function as a persuasion plan, not just a list of lines.

### Strategy Review
- Is there a clear objective?
- Is the target audience visible in the reasoning?
- Is one dominant message established?

### Hook Review
- Are hook options relevant and strong?
- Is the recommended hook clearly justified?
- Does the hook sound speakable and native?

### Structure Review
- Is the script organized logically?
- Does the viewer move from attention to belief to action?
- Is the script too crowded or too vague?

### Proof Review
- Does the script clearly specify what must be shown?
- Are strong claims tied to proof moments?
- Does the script avoid unsupported hype?

### Objection Review
- Is the main hesitation identified?
- Is it answered naturally in the script?

### CTA Review
- Is the CTA connected to the strongest benefit or proof?
- Does it feel earned?

### Execution Review
- Does the script sound natural out loud?
- Can a creator or editor use it directly?
- Does it support the next storyboard or prompt step?

#### Red Flags
- feature dumping
- brand-copy tone
- too many equal-weight selling points
- no proof path
- CTA disconnected from the body
- lines that are readable but not speakable

---

## 3.3 Storyboard Planning Checklist

A strong storyboard should turn persuasion into visual execution.

### Scene Purpose Review
- Does every scene have a commercial job?
- Are there filler scenes?
- Is the scene order easy to follow?

### Product Visibility Review
- Does the product appear early enough?
- Is it clearly readable?
- Is its use understandable?

### Proof Review
- Are the proof scenes obvious?
- Do visual actions support the script’s main claim?

### Hook Review
- Does the opening scene visually stop the scroll?
- Is it understandable without heavy explanation?

### Overlay Review
- Do text overlays support rather than clutter?
- Are they tied to important beats?

### Pacing Review
- Are proof scenes given enough time?
- Is the storyboard too fast to understand or too slow for short-form use?

### CTA Review
- Does the ending visually support the CTA?
- Is the final action moment clear?

### Production Review
- Is the storyboard practical to film?
- Is it clear enough for AI generation if needed?

#### Red Flags
- scene list without scene purpose
- product appears too late
- visual beauty with no proof logic
- overcomplicated transitions
- CTA scene disconnected from previous scenes

---

## 3.4 Prompt Conversion Checklist

A strong prompt should produce a commercially usable video, not just a cinematic one.

### Objective Review
- Is the commercial goal clear?
- Is the intended style specified?

### Product Definition Review
- Is the product clearly described?
- Are consistency needs included?

### Scene Logic Review
- Is the prompt structured in understandable sequence?
- Are the key scenes commercially meaningful?

### Proof Review
- Does the prompt include visible proof events?
- Can the model actually render the key benefit clearly?

### Creator / Subject Review
- If a person is present, is their role and behavior defined clearly?
- Does the creator style match the intended format?

### Environment Review
- Does the setting support the product and message?
- Is it not distracting or irrelevant?

### Constraint Review
- Are negative constraints useful and not excessive?
- Is the prompt overloaded?

### Practicality Review
- Would this prompt likely generate something usable for commerce?
- Is the model being asked to do too many incompatible things?

#### Red Flags
- abstract marketing language
- no visible proof event
- weak product consistency
- no sequence clarity
- too cinematic for the use case
- overly dense prompt with conflicting instructions

---

## 3.5 Reference Adaptation Checklist

A strong adaptation should preserve strategy, not copy surface detail.

### Reference Understanding Review
- Does the output identify what actually makes the reference effective?
- Is the strategic engine named clearly?

### Product Fit Review
- Does it test whether the user’s product actually fits the reference logic?
- Does it avoid forcing weak matches?

### Transfer Logic Review
- Are transferable elements clearly separated from must-change elements?
- Is the adaptation commercially grounded?

### Originality Review
- Does the new direction feel meaningfully distinct?
- Are variants actually different from one another?

### Downstream Use Review
- Can the adaptation be turned into a script, storyboard, or prompt cleanly?

#### Red Flags
- one-to-one imitation
- variants that are only minor wording changes
- no fit assessment
- carries over reference-specific proof that does not apply
- surface copying disguised as adaptation

---

## 3.6 Variant Planning Checklist

A strong variant plan should help the user learn something, not just create more options.

### Test Logic Review
- Is the testing goal clearly defined?
- Is the shared core stable enough for comparison?

### Variant Difference Review
- Are variants meaningfully different?
- Is the changing variable clear?

### Hypothesis Review
- Does each variant have a clear reason for existing?
- Is it obvious what performance question each variant is testing?

### Comparison Review
- Is there a useful test order?
- Are fair test constraints identified?

### Production Review
- Can these variants actually be executed?

#### Red Flags
- random variations with no test logic
- too many changing variables at once
- no shared core
- no hypothesis
- no explanation of what results would mean

## 4. Output Quality Scoring Model (Optional)

Teams may use a simple 1–5 or 1–10 score across these categories:

- Hook quality
- Product clarity
- Proof strength
- Objection handling
- CTA fit
- Platform-native feel
- Execution readiness

Example 1–5 scale:
- 1 = poor
- 2 = weak
- 3 = acceptable
- 4 = strong
- 5 = excellent

This is optional, but useful for QA and benchmarking.

## 5. Cross-Stage Consistency Checklist

If outputs are chained across multiple steps, verify consistency between them.

### Script → Storyboard
- Does the storyboard preserve the script’s main promise?
- Are proof beats visually represented?
- Is the CTA still supported?

### Storyboard → Prompt
- Does the prompt preserve scene order and proof logic?
- Does the product remain clearly defined?
- Does the format style stay aligned?

### Reference Adaptation → Script
- Does the script reflect the adaptation strategy?
- Is originality preserved?
- Is product fit stronger than reference similarity?

### Variant Planning → Production Assets
- Are the tested differences still visible in the produced outputs?
- Has the shared core stayed stable?

#### Red Flags
- logic breaks between stages
- product role changes unintentionally
- tone drifts between outputs
- proof gets lost during conversion
- CTA weakens later in the chain

---

## 6. Common Failure Patterns

### Pattern 1: Structurally Complete but Commercially Empty
The output fills all sections but does not help conversion or understanding.

### Pattern 2: Creative but Unusable
The output sounds exciting but is too vague to execute.

### Pattern 3: Product-Invisible Content
The format is strong, but the product is not clearly understood or remembered.

### Pattern 4: Proof-Poor Selling
The output claims strong benefits without enough evidence.

### Pattern 5: Overloaded Message
Too many points compete for attention in a short format.

### Pattern 6: CTA Mismatch
The ask is stronger than the persuasion that came before it.

## 7. Checklist by User Perspective

### For a Seller
Ask:
- Will this help sell my product?
- Is the message clear?
- Can I hand this to someone and get it made?

### For a Creator
Ask:
- Can I realistically say this?
- Do I know what to film?
- Does this sound like me or like an ad script?

### For an Editor
Ask:
- Do I know what the important beats are?
- Is proof clear enough to prioritize in the edit?
- Is pacing guidance usable?

### For an AI Operator
Ask:
- Is the prompt concrete enough?
- Is the product defined clearly?
- Can the model likely produce the right type of scene?

### For a Strategist
Ask:
- Is the persuasion logic visible?
- Are the tradeoffs and priorities clear?
- Can I improve this or compare it across iterations?

---

## 8. Minimum Acceptable Quality Standard

An output should not be considered production-usable unless it at least:
- identifies a clear product role
- establishes one main message
- includes or preserves proof logic
- addresses at least one major objection when relevant
- contains a CTA that makes sense for the persuasion level
- is usable by the next workflow stage

Anything below that may be informational, but not operational.

---

## Final Principle
A good output is not only well-structured.

It should be:
- commercially meaningful
- strategically sound
- easy to use
- faithful to product truth
- ready for the next step

The strongest outputs in this system do not just look complete.

They make better creative decisions easier.
