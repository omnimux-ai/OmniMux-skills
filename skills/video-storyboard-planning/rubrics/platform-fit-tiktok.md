# TikTok Platform Fit Rubric

> Purpose: Evaluate whether a storyboard, commerce concept, or short-form video idea is truly native to TikTok rather than merely adapted from generic advertising. This rubric focuses on attention capture, feed behavior, creator-like authenticity, editing logic, participation in platform norms, and short-form retention dynamics.

## Scoring Overview

- Score each dimension from **1–5**
- Apply weights to compute a **weighted total out of 100**
- Check **Critical Platform-Fit Failures** before approval
- Interpretation:
  - **90–100** = Exceptionally TikTok-native
  - **75–89** = Strong TikTok fit
  - **60–74** = Usable but somewhat generic or platform-loose
  - **Below 60** = Weak platform fit; likely feels like a repurposed ad

### Score meanings

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Not TikTok-native; actively mismatched to the platform |
| 2 | Weak fit; some platform signals but mostly generic |
| 3 | Acceptable fit; recognizable short-form logic but average |
| 4 | Strong fit; clearly designed for TikTok behavior |
| 5 | Excellent fit; deeply native, high-retention, and creator-aligned |

## 1. Scroll-Stopping Opening (Weight: 14)

**What this checks:**
Whether the first moment is built for feed interruption and immediate viewer attention.

### 1
- Opening is slow, polished in the wrong way, or context-heavy
- Feels like a conventional ad intro
- Weak chance of stopping a scroll

### 2
- Attempts a hook, but it is generic or low urgency
- Some stopping power, but likely easy to skip

### 3
- Functional opener
- Clear enough to register, but not especially strong

### 4
- Strong opening beat
- Immediate curiosity, tension, surprise, pain, payoff, or relevance

### 5
- Excellent thumb-stop energy
- Opening is instantly legible, platform-native, and hard to ignore

**Reviewer prompts**
- Would this stop a cold user in-feed?
- Is the hook immediate enough for TikTok?
- Does the first beat earn the next second?

---

## 2. Native Short-Form Pacing (Weight: 12)

**What this checks:**
Whether the storyboard is paced like TikTok rather than long-form, TV, or static branded content.

### 1
- Pacing is slow, bloated, or structurally mismatched to short-form
- Scenes linger without adding value

### 2
- Some short-form logic exists, but rhythm is uneven or too traditional
- Momentum drops noticeably

### 3
- Reasonable short-form pacing
- Usable but not especially sharp

### 4
- Strong pacing discipline
- Scenes advance quickly and purposefully

### 5
- Excellent TikTok rhythm
- Tight progression, high information efficiency, and sustained momentum without feeling rushed

**Reviewer prompts**
- Does it move at a TikTok-appropriate speed?
- Are beats compact and useful?
- Is there any dead air in the concept?

---

## 3. Creator-Like Authenticity (Weight: 12)

**What this checks:**
Whether the idea feels like content a real creator or seller would post natively on TikTok.

### 1
- Feels corporate, over-scripted, or obviously repurposed
- Lacks creator energy entirely

### 2
- Contains minor creator-like elements, but still reads as a traditional ad
- Authenticity is superficial

### 3
- Moderately authentic
- Some creator-native feeling is present

### 4
- Strong creator fit
- Tone, setup, and delivery feel natural to user-generated or seller-generated content

### 5
- Exceptionally authentic
- Feels like it belongs in a real TikTok feed and could plausibly perform as organic content

**Reviewer prompts**
- Would this feel natural coming from a creator or shop account?
- Is the tone human and feed-native?
- Does it avoid the “obvious ad” feeling enough?

---

## 4. Feed Behavior & Retention Logic (Weight: 12)

**What this checks:**
Whether the content is built around how viewers actually consume TikTok: fast judgment, curiosity gaps, escalating payoff, and minimal friction.

### 1
- No meaningful retention design
- Viewer likely drops quickly after the opener

### 2
- Some retention ideas exist, but sequencing is weak
- Curiosity or payoff fades too early

### 3
- Basic retention structure
- Beginning, middle, and end are present

### 4
- Strong retention logic
- Information and visuals are sequenced to keep the user watching

### 5
- Excellent feed-aware construction
- Every beat creates momentum toward the next with high viewer efficiency

**Reviewer prompts**
- Why would someone keep watching after the first beat?
- Is there a sequence of mini-payoffs?
- Does the concept understand feed impatience?

**What this checks:**
Whether the visual style, framing, and scene design fit TikTok conventions rather than generic branded video grammar.

### 1
- Visual language feels like TVC, website brand film, or static ad adaptation
- Wrong framing or editorial sensibility for TikTok

### 2
- Some platform-relevant visuals, but execution still feels transplanted
- Limited vertical-native thinking

### 3
- Generally compatible with TikTok
- Some scenes still feel generic

### 4
- Strong vertical-native visual thinking
- Framing, shot selection, and visual emphasis feel platform-appropriate

### 5
- Excellent TikTok-native visual language
- Scenes feel built for handheld, creator-style, fast-clarity mobile viewing

**Reviewer prompts**
- Does this visually feel like TikTok?
- Is vertical/mobile attention behavior reflected in the shot design?
- Are scenes optimized for small-screen comprehension?

## 6. Spoken / On-Screen Communication Style (Weight: 8)

### 1
- Language feels formal, ad-like, or unnatural
- Copy is bloated or too polished

### 2
- Some directness exists, but tone is still off-platform
- Communication style feels written more than spoken

### 3
- Understandable and acceptable
- Mostly suitable for short-form

### 4
- Strong TikTok communication style
- Direct, efficient, and easy to absorb quickly

### 5
- Excellent platform-native voice
- Conversational, sharp, and highly suited to feed consumption and creator delivery

**Reviewer prompts**
- Does the language sound like something a person would actually say on TikTok?
- Is it easy to process quickly?
- Are captions or lines concise and effective?

## 7. Participation in Familiar TikTok Patterns (Weight: 8)

**What this checks:**
Whether the concept intelligently uses recognizable TikTok structures without becoming stale.

### 1
- Ignores platform patterns entirely
- Feels detached from user expectations on TikTok

### 2
- Slight use of familiar patterns, but weak or awkward
- Feels imitative without understanding

### 3
- Uses at least one recognizable TikTok structure competently
- Familiar but standard

### 4
- Good use of platform-native patterns
- Format choices support performance and comprehension

### 5
- Excellent use of TikTok conventions with strategic freshness
- Recognizable enough to feel native, distinct enough to feel worth watching

**Examples of patterns**
- POV setup
- “You need this if…”
- Problem → reveal → payoff
- Fast demo with reaction
- Comparison / test format
- Day-in-the-life integration
- Honest review framing
- Comment-response structure
- “I didn’t expect this…” framing

**Reviewer prompts**
- Is the concept using a TikTok-familiar structure?
- Does it feel native rather than copied?
- Does the chosen pattern help retention and selling?

---

## Critical Platform-Fit Failures

If any of the following are true, the concept should not be approved without revision:

1. **The opening is too slow or weak for feed behavior**
2. **The concept feels like a conventional ad rather than TikTok content**
3. **Pacing is not suitable for short-form viewing**
4. **The communication style feels overly formal, polished, or non-conversational**
5. **Visual grammar is not optimized for vertical/mobile-first consumption**
6. **Commerce integration feels forced or unnatural**
7. **There is little reason for a viewer to keep watching after the first beat**

## Weighted Scoring Formula

```text
weighted_points = (dimension_score / 5) * dimension_weight
overall_score_100 = sum(all weighted_points)
```

### Example

```text
Scroll-Stopping Opening score = 4
Weight = 14
Weighted points = (4/5) * 14 = 11.2
```

## Approval Heuristics

### Approve
Use when:
- No critical platform-fit failures
- Score is 85+
- Hook, pacing, authenticity, and retention logic are all strong

### Approve with Minor Revisions
Use when:
- No critical platform-fit failures
- Score is 70–84
- Overall TikTok fit is solid, but some elements feel slightly generic or under-optimized

### Revise
Use when:
- Score is 50–69
- Or any key area scores 1–2 in opening, pacing, authenticity, or retention logic

### Rebuild
Use when:
- Score is below 50
- Or multiple critical platform-fit failures exist
- Concept fundamentally does not behave like TikTok-native content

## What Strong TikTok Platform Fit Usually Looks Like

A strong TikTok-native storyboard usually:

- Starts with an immediate, legible hook
- Moves quickly without feeling chaotic
- Feels like something a creator, seller, or relatable person would genuinely post
- Uses mobile-first visual framing and direct language
- Maintains curiosity or payoff momentum across the sequence
- Integrates product selling in a way that feels normal for social commerce
- Reflects familiar platform patterns while still feeling fresh
- Creates at least some reason to react, share, or keep watching

---

## Common Platform-Fit Failure Patterns

1. **Repurposed ad syndrome**
   - Looks like a commercial trimmed into vertical

2. **Overproduced in the wrong way**
   - Too polished, too distant, too brand-safe to feel native

3. **Slow setup**
   - Takes too long to arrive at the point

4. **No retention architecture**
   - Hook exists, but the middle collapses

5. **Formal copywriting voice**
   - Sounds written for a deck, not spoken in-feed

6. **Trend mimicry without fluency**
   - Uses surface-level TikTok cues without behavioral understanding

7. **Forced selling**
   - Product insertion breaks the natural content flow

## Revision Guidance by Weak Area

### If Scroll-Stopping Opening is weak
- Move the most surprising or relevant moment to frame one
- Remove setup and context-loading
- Use visible tension, proof, or relatable pain immediately

### If Native Short-Form Pacing is weak
- Compress scenes
- Remove redundant beats
- Make each moment advance understanding, curiosity, or desire

### If Creator-Like Authenticity is weak
- Reduce polished ad language
- Increase human viewpoint, lived context, or casual realism
- Make delivery feel spoken, not scripted for a brand deck

### If Retention Logic is weak
- Create sequential payoffs
- Use mini reveals, comparison, or escalating proof
- Give each beat a reason to exist

### If TikTok-Native Visual Language is weak
- Shift toward vertical-friendly, handheld, direct framing
- Prioritize immediate readability on mobile
- Avoid generic commercial composition habits

### If Commerce Integration is weak
- Make product discovery part of the content logic
- Use demo, reaction, problem-solving, or personal recommendation framing
- Avoid abrupt “buy this now” insertions without build-up

### If Communication Style is weak
- Rewrite lines into natural spoken language
- Shorten on-screen text
- Favor directness and clarity over polish

## LLM Self-Check Questions

Before approving a TikTok storyboard, ask:

1. Would this feel native in an actual TikTok feed?
2. Is the first second strong enough to compete for attention?
3. Does the sequence move with real short-form momentum?
4. Does it sound like a person on TikTok, not a traditional ad?
5. Is the product integrated naturally into platform behavior?
6. Is there a reason to keep watching after the hook?

If any answer is “no,” revise before approval.
