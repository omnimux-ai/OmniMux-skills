# E-commerce Conversion Rubric

> Purpose: Evaluate whether a video storyboard or short-form commerce concept is likely to drive product understanding, desire, trust, and action. This rubric is optimized for TikTok Shop, DTC short video, paid social commerce creatives, and creator-led selling content.

## Scoring Overview

- Score each dimension from **1–5**
- Multiply by weights to calculate a **weighted total out of 100**
- Check **Critical Conversion Failures** before approval
- Interpretation:
  - **90–100** = Exceptional conversion potential
  - **75–89** = Strong conversion potential
  - **60–74** = Mixed / needs improvement
  - **Below 60** = Weak conversion potential

### Score meanings

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Fails the dimension; unlikely to convert |
| 2 | Weak; major conversion gap |
| 3 | Adequate; some persuasive value but average |
| 4 | Strong; likely to support conversion |
| 5 | Excellent; highly persuasive and buyer-relevant |

## 1. Problem-Solution Clarity (Weight: 12)

**What this checks:**
Whether the content clearly identifies a pain point, desire, or use case and connects the product to solving it.

### 1
- No clear problem, need, or use context
- Product appears without meaningful reason
- Viewer may not understand why anyone would buy it

### 2
- Problem or desire is hinted at but vague
- Product-solution relationship is weak
- Content feels more presentational than persuasive

### 3
- Basic problem-solution framing exists
- Viewer can infer why the product matters
- Reason to care is adequate but not sharp

### 4
- Strong clarity on need and solution
- Product is positioned around a recognizable buyer job
- Relevance is easy to grasp

### 5
- Exceptionally clear problem-solution fit
- Buyer need feels immediate, specific, and well matched to the product
- Content makes purchase logic feel obvious

**Reviewer prompts**
- What problem or desire is being addressed?
- Is the product clearly shown as the answer?
- Would a likely buyer immediately understand the use case?

---

## 2. Product Understanding Speed (Weight: 10)

**What this checks:**
How quickly a viewer can understand what the product is, what category it belongs to, and what it basically does.

### 1
- Viewer cannot quickly tell what the product is
- Identity, function, or category is confusing

### 2
- Product becomes clear only after delay or effort
- Initial presentation is ambiguous or too stylized

### 3
- Product is understandable within a reasonable amount of time
- Some clarity, but not maximally efficient

### 4
- Product is understood quickly and cleanly
- Function and category are legible early

### 5
- Product understanding is nearly instant
- Viewer knows what it is, who it is for, and what it does within the opening beats

**Reviewer prompts**
- How many seconds until the viewer “gets it”?
- Is the product shown clearly enough, early enough?
- Is visual communication doing enough of the work?

---

## 3. Core Benefit Communication (Weight: 12)

**What this checks:**
Whether the most important value proposition is communicated clearly and memorably.

### 1
- Benefits are absent, unclear, or generic
- Viewer may see the product but not the value

### 2
- Benefit mention exists, but weak or broad
- Value proposition lacks sharpness or memorability

### 3
- Main benefit is understandable
- Persuasive impact is moderate

### 4
- Benefits are clear, relevant, and well integrated
- Content makes the value easy to remember

### 5
- Main value proposition is extremely clear and compelling
- Benefits feel specific, buyer-relevant, and hard to ignore

**Reviewer prompts**
- What is the one main reason to buy this?
- Is that reason communicated clearly?
- Will a viewer remember the core benefit after scrolling away?

---

## 4. Buyer Relevance & Audience Match (Weight: 10)

**What this checks:**
How precisely the content reflects the target buyer’s priorities, motivations, habits, and context.

### 1
- No clear sense of audience
- Messaging feels random, generic, or mismatched

### 2
- Broad audience fit, but not convincing
- Some relevance exists, but weakly expressed

### 3
- Plausible fit for an intended buyer
- Audience logic is acceptable but not highly tuned

### 4
- Strong audience alignment
- Pain points, identity cues, or use scenarios match likely buyers

### 5
- Exceptional buyer fit
- Content appears built for a specific audience mindset and purchase trigger
- Highly likely to resonate with the intended shopper

**Reviewer prompts**
- Who is this for?
- Does the content reflect that buyer’s real concerns or desires?
- Does the tone and angle feel buyer-native?

---

## 5. Trust & Believability (Weight: 10)

**What this checks:**
Whether the creative feels credible, grounded, and likely to be believed.

### 1
- Feels fake, exaggerated, or suspicious
- Viewer trust likely drops

### 2
- Some credibility issues or overclaiming tone
- Trust signals are weak or inconsistent

### 3
- Generally believable
- Not especially trust-building, but acceptable

### 4
- Good credibility
- Product feels real, usable, and honestly represented

### 5
- Strong trust-building effect
- Presentation feels authentic, confident, and grounded in believable use or proof

**Reviewer prompts**
- Would a skeptical buyer believe this?
- Does the execution feel authentic or overproduced in a harmful way?
- Are any claims implied without support?

---

## 6. Proof, Demonstration & Evidence (Weight: 12)

**What this checks:**
How effectively the content proves the product works or shows value in action.

### 1
- No meaningful proof or demonstration
- Content relies on empty assertions

### 2
- Minimal proof
- Demonstration exists but is unclear, weak, or irrelevant

### 3
- Some evidence or demonstration is present
- Supports persuasion at a basic level

### 4
- Strong demonstration or evidence
- Product value is shown, not just stated

### 5
- Excellent proof structure
- Demonstration is clear, convincing, visual, and directly tied to purchase motivation

**Reviewer prompts**
- What proof does the buyer actually see?
- Is the demonstration relevant to the claimed benefit?
- Does the content reduce uncertainty about product performance?

---

## 7. Objection Handling (Weight: 8)

**What this checks:**
Whether the content anticipates and reduces common buyer hesitations.

### 1
- Ignores obvious objections
- Buyer uncertainty remains high

### 2
- Slight awareness of objections, but weak handling
- Key concerns are left unresolved

### 3
- At least one likely objection is indirectly addressed
- Adequate but limited reassurance

### 4
- Good objection handling
- Content reduces friction around common doubts

### 5
- Excellent objection management
- Key concerns are proactively and naturally neutralized through visuals, framing, or proof

**Common objections may include**
- “Does it actually work?”
- “Is it easy to use?”
- “Is it worth the price?”
- “Will it fit my situation/body/home/routine?”
- “Is this just another gimmick?”

**Reviewer prompts**
- What would stop a buyer from purchasing?
- Does the content answer that concern?
- Is reassurance built into the scenes?

---

## 8. Desire, Urgency & Motivation (Weight: 8)

**What this checks:**
Whether the content makes the viewer want the product now rather than merely understand it.

### 1
- No desire or urgency created
- Viewer may understand the product but feel little motivation

### 2
- Some interest, but low emotional pull
- Motivation to act is weak

### 3
- Moderate desire or motivation
- Adequate persuasive energy

### 4
- Strong desire or momentum
- Viewer is given emotional and practical reasons to care now

### 5
- Excellent motivational pull
- Product feels timely, desirable, and worth acting on immediately

**Reviewer prompts**
- Does the content create wanting, not just awareness?
- Is there emotional energy behind the pitch?
- Why would the viewer act now instead of later?

---

## 9. Offer / Reason to Act (Weight: 6)

**What this checks:**
Whether the content includes or supports a compelling reason to take the next step.

### 1
- No offer logic or action incentive
- Content ends without a reason to move

### 2
- Weak or generic reason to act
- Incentive feels disconnected or forgettable

### 3
- A reasonable reason to act exists
- Functional but not especially persuasive

### 4
- Good action incentive
- Viewer has a meaningful reason to click or buy

### 5
- Strong and compelling reason to act
- Offer logic meaningfully boosts conversion intent

**Examples**
- Limited-time deal
- Bundle value
- Convenience payoff
- Problem avoidance
- Social proof momentum
- Availability cue
- Shop-native impulse trigger

**Reviewer prompts**
- Why should the buyer act now?
- Is there an explicit or implicit incentive?
- Does the reason to act match the funnel stage?

---

## 10. CTA Quality (Weight: 6)

**What this checks:**
Whether the call to action is clear, well-timed, and conversion-supportive.

### 1
- No CTA or confusing CTA
- Buyer is not guided to the next action

### 2
- CTA exists but is weak, generic, or abrupt

### 3
- CTA is present and understandable
- Functional but ordinary

### 4
- Good CTA
- Clear next step and good alignment with the content

### 5
- Excellent CTA
- Feels natural, persuasive, and properly earned by the preceding sequence

**Reviewer prompts**
- Is the next action obvious?
- Does the CTA fit the evidence and momentum built in the creative?
- Does the ending help conversion or just stop the video?

---

## 11. Conversion Flow & Friction Reduction (Weight: 4)

**What this checks:**
Whether the content reduces confusion and supports a smooth path from attention to action.

### 1
- High friction; confusing sequence
- Buyer effort remains high

### 2
- Several friction points remain
- Flow is not optimized for conversion

### 3
- Reasonably smooth
- Some unnecessary friction or ambiguity

### 4
- Smooth conversion logic
- Content reduces uncertainty and cognitive load well

### 5
- Excellent flow
- Creative guides the viewer through a low-friction decision path with strong clarity

**Reviewer prompts**
- Does the video reduce or increase buyer hesitation?
- Are there points of confusion?
- Does the order of information help the purchase decision?

---

## 12. Commercial Focus vs Distraction (Weight: 2)

**What this checks:**
Whether the creative remains focused on selling outcomes rather than unrelated entertainment or visual excess.

### 1
- Distracting, self-indulgent, or off-topic
- Commerce objective is buried

### 2
- Some useful selling content, but too much distraction

### 3
- Balanced enough
- Commercial point remains visible

### 4
- Strong commercial focus
- Creative elements support selling rather than compete with it

### 5
- Highly disciplined commercial execution
- Every major element contributes to conversion

**Reviewer prompts**
- Is anything stealing attention from the product?
- Does style serve selling?
- Is the commerce objective sustained throughout?

---

## Critical Conversion Failures

If any of these are true, the concept should not be approved without revision:

1. **The viewer cannot quickly understand what the product is**
2. **The main benefit is unclear**
3. **No meaningful proof or demonstration exists**
4. **The content creates little or no reason to buy**
5. **CTA is absent or too weak for a conversion brief**
6. **The concept feels misleading, unbelievable, or trust-damaging**
7. **The product is visually secondary to irrelevant creative elements**
8. **The sequence increases confusion instead of reducing it**

## Weighted Scoring Formula

```text
weighted_points = (dimension_score / 5) * dimension_weight
overall_score_100 = sum(all weighted_points)
```

### Example

```text
Core Benefit Communication score = 4
Weight = 12
Weighted points = (4/5) * 12 = 9.6
```

---

## Approval Heuristics

### Approve
Use when:
- No critical conversion failures
- Score is 85+
- Product clarity, benefit communication, proof, and CTA are all strong

### Approve with Minor Revisions
Use when:
- No critical conversion failures
- Score is 70–84
- Conversion logic is solid, but a few persuasive gaps remain

### Revise
Use when:
- Score is 50–69
- Or any core area scores 1–2 in product understanding, benefit communication, proof, or CTA

### Rebuild
Use when:
- Score is below 50
- Or multiple critical conversion failures exist
- Concept is too weak or too unclear to salvage incrementally

## What Strong Conversion Content Usually Includes

High-converting e-commerce storyboards often do at least most of the following:

- Make the product legible immediately
- Show a clear use case or pain point
- Communicate one dominant benefit clearly
- Demonstrate the product in action
- Build trust through grounded visuals or realistic framing
- Reduce objections before the buyer consciously formulates them
- Create desire, convenience, relief, confidence, or urgency
- End with a clear, action-supportive CTA

---

## Common Conversion Failure Patterns

1. **Aesthetic-first, commerce-second**
   - Beautiful content that never really sells

2. **Feature listing without buyer meaning**
   - Information exists, but no persuasive translation into value

3. **No proof**
   - Product is talked about, not demonstrated

4. **Late clarity**
   - Buyer still doesn’t understand the item too far into the video

5. **Weak motivation**
   - Viewer thinks “okay,” not “I want this”

6. **Trust leak**
   - Feels exaggerated, gimmicky, or suspicious

7. **No friction reduction**
   - Buyer is left with obvious unanswered questions

8. **Tacked-on CTA**
   - The ask arrives without being earned

## Revision Guidance by Weak Area

### If Product Understanding Speed is weak
- Show the product clearly in the first beats
- Reduce abstract opening visuals
- Use stronger category and function cues

### If Core Benefit Communication is weak
- Focus on one dominant value proposition
- Translate features into buyer outcomes
- Remove secondary clutter

### If Proof is weak
- Add visible demo moments
- Show before/after context carefully where appropriate and compliant
- Use interaction, result, comparison, or usability evidence

### If Trust is weak
- Tone down hype
- Make use context more realistic
- Remove unsupported implication and make visuals more grounded

### If Objection Handling is weak
- Identify the top 1–2 buyer hesitations
- Build reassurance into scenes, not just captions
- Show ease, fit, outcome, or practicality

### If Desire / Motivation is weak
- Increase emotional relevance
- Make the payoff feel immediate and personal
- Clarify why this product matters now

### If CTA is weak
- Make the next step explicit
- Reinforce the key benefit before the ask
- Align CTA with user intent and platform behavior

## LLM Self-Check Questions

Before finalizing a commerce storyboard, ask:

1. Would a cold viewer quickly know what the product is?
2. Is the main reason to buy clear?
3. Is there visible proof, not just description?
4. Does the concept reduce doubt and increase desire?
5. Is there a real reason to act now?
6. Does the ending clearly convert attention into the next step?

If any answer is “no,” revise before approval.
